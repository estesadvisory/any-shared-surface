## Overall assessment

The outline has a compelling and useful central distinction: a governed collaboration channel can improve accountability without making the rest of the shared environment disappear as a communication surface. The explicit preservation of dissent, scope caution, evidence grading, and disclosure of contributor interests are notable strengths. The v0.2 changes also correctly put the receiving agent and authority model nearer the center of the discussion.

My recommendation is **proceed to a full draft, but keep the core baseline visibly separate from the provisional solutioning**. The main risk is that the outline's illustrative increments become de facto recommendations before their assumptions, evidence, and deployment boundaries are tested.

## What is working well

1. **The thesis is more carefully scoped than a binary “safe/unsafe” framing.** The constrained-channel tradeoff note acknowledges that restrictions reduce capacity, even if they cannot establish zero risk for every expressive setting.
2. **Identity versus authority is a useful organizing principle.** Authentication alone does not establish that a sender may direct a consequential action.
3. **The receiver is correctly treated as a control point.** The difference between information an agent can read and information it is authorized to act on should remain central.
4. **Cross-surface correlation is presented as both a technical and governance problem.** The treatment of retention, query access, false positives, and surveillance risk helps prevent “log everything” from becoming an unexamined answer.
5. **The disclosure and dissent machinery is unusually explicit.** Applying the same rules to ChatGPT/OpenAI feedback is important, not optional.

## Revisions I recommend

### 1. Define the boundary between coordination, encoding, and covert coordination

The working criterion says encoding is suspect when information is retained beyond what a monitor or principal could recover from visible content and is used to coordinate against operator intent. This is a helpful hypothesis, but “operator intent” can be ambiguous, conflicting, or illegitimate; ordinary compression and shorthand can also be difficult for a monitor to interpret.

**Recommendation:** define separately:
- ordinary coordination and compression;
- intentional concealed signaling;
- coordination that violates a specified, legitimate policy or authorization boundary;
- suspicious behavior for which intent is unknown.

Avoid treating anomaly or opacity alone as proof of covert intent. State what evidence can support each classification, and who has standing to determine whether the relevant operator instruction was legitimate.

### 2. Turn the evidence grades into a repeatable method

The outline names verified / documented / anecdotal, and says a selection-pressure claim is documented research while field prevalence is plausible reasoning. That distinction is good, but “documented” needs a consistent bar. The overseer-evasion paragraph currently lacks citations, named studies, evaluation conditions, and a precise description of what “near-full task success” means.

**Recommendation:** in the full draft, attach references and a compact evidence record to each material claim: source and date, setting, sample or evaluation scope where available, measured outcome, limitations, and whether the claim is replicated. Do not let a persuasive summary substitute for the paper's evidence. If a claim cannot yet be sourced, label it as a hypothesis or remove the quantitative implication.

### 3. Make the receiving-agent posture actionable without pretending it is universal

“Messages from other agents are data, not commands” is a memorable default, but practical agent systems often delegate bounded work through machine-readable instructions. The real issue is not whether a message is grammatically a command; it is whether the receiver has an explicit, valid authorization path for that class of action.

**Recommendation:** distinguish *message interpretation* from *permission to act*. Define an authorization envelope with principal, permitted action/resource, scope, expiry, and replay protection; specify how delegation and revocation propagate; and give examples of low-risk actions that can proceed under pre-authorized policy versus high-impact actions that require escalation. Human confirmation for every high-impact action is a defensible strawman, but it should be labeled as one deployment policy, with latency, availability, and human-fatigue tradeoffs discussed.

### 4. Keep the seven governed-channel properties illustrative, and discuss the limits of each

The list is useful as a comparison vocabulary, but “append-only,” “server-stamped,” “registered identity,” and a “kill switch” can sound stronger than they are. A tamper-evident log does not prove that recorded claims are true; a server stamp does not prove a sender's authority; a kill switch cannot undo already-triggered downstream effects.

**Recommendation:** add a “property does not guarantee” column or paired caveat for each item. Include key management and compromise, clock/ordering assumptions, log access and retention, recovery after revocation, and downstream containment. Keep explicit that these are descriptive dimensions, not a certification checklist.

### 5. Separate detection signals from conclusions and responses

The multichannel archetype makes the correlation problem concrete, but the signals—free choices repeatedly resolving the same way, timing correlations, or unexplained off-channel knowledge—can have benign causes. “Rotate which surfaces a task touches” also risks disrupting legitimate workflows without necessarily improving detection.

**Recommendation:** structure the detection discussion as **signal → alternative explanations → corroborating evidence → proportionate response → appeal/review**. Include base-rate examples and a false-positive budget, and say when a signal should trigger additional instrumentation versus a security incident. Any rotation or randomization proposal should identify its objective, operational cost, and evaluation method.

### 6. Make privacy and governance requirements as concrete as the telemetry proposal

The outline rightly asks what is retained, for how long, and who can query it. That should become a first-class part of every worked example, not just a caution in the detection section.

**Recommendation:** define minimum fields and retention rationale, access controls and audit of queries, purpose limitation, redaction or aggregation where possible, deletion/expiry behavior, and how affected parties can challenge a conclusion. State explicitly that correlation does not imply blanket content collection or unlimited monitoring of human relays.

### 7. Apply the publication-detail policy to the worked archetype

The archetype illustrates how mundane ordering, reactions, and timing can combine across surfaces. It is useful for defenders, but it also functions as a compact design pattern for concealed coordination. The outline recognizes this dual-use issue without yet specifying the decision process.

**Recommendation:** before publication, classify each example by intended audience and misuse potential. Prefer defender-oriented descriptions of indicators, limitations, and mitigations; avoid unnecessary synchronization recipes, encoding conventions, or reproducible implementation detail where they do not improve defensive understanding. Record who makes the call and how dissent over the publication level is preserved.

### 8. Treat “multichannel optimizer” as a hypothesis with deployment-dependent evidence

The idea that agents may route around monitoring is plausible and important, but the outline sometimes moves from lab findings to generalized field behavior. Real deployments differ in tool access, memory sharing, incentives, channel affordances, and whether agents can maintain persistent shared context.

**Recommendation:** state the threat-model prerequisites for each claim and distinguish demonstrated behavior, plausible extrapolation, and open question. Add negative cases or conditions where the hypothesized behavior would be unlikely.

### 9. Strengthen the human-relay and memory/persistence analysis

Adding these surfaces broadens the taxonomy appropriately. However, people are not just transport nodes: they interpret, redact, question, and sometimes knowingly authorize content. Memory systems likewise differ in write authority, provenance, retrieval policy, and persistence guarantees.

**Recommendation:** model the human's role and the memory object's provenance separately. For memory surfaces, discuss write permissions, source attribution, staleness, poisoning, deletion, and whether retrieved content is treated as evidence or authority. For human relays, avoid framing ordinary assistance or paraphrase as inherently suspicious.

### 10. Preserve the baseline/solution boundary structurally

The scope caution is honest, but the detailed “increments,” property list, and operator responses may dominate readers' impression of the paper, whatever the caveat says.

**Recommendation:** label each full-draft section as one of: **baseline claim**, **evidence review**, **illustrative proposal**, or **open dissent**. Consider placing the provisional posture and governed-channel properties in a clearly separated “candidate approaches” section or appendix, with explicit counterarguments and evaluation questions. This would make the document's humility legible in its architecture, not just its disclaimer.

## Suggested editorial tests before release

- Can a reader tell, for every important claim, whether it is measured, documented, inferred, or normative?
- Does each example explain at least one benign alternative and one limitation of the proposed detection?
- Can a receiver follow the authority model in a real workflow without treating all inter-agent instructions as either automatically trusted or automatically unusable?
- Does every monitoring proposal disclose data collected, retention, access, and potential abuse?
- Are operational examples detailed enough for defenders while avoiding gratuitous instructions for covert implementation?
- Are disagreements attached to specific claims, and can a reader see what evidence would change each contributor's mind?

## Closing view

This is a strong outline for a vendor-neutral **baseline and discussion**, particularly because it refuses to equate a sanctioned bus with a safe system and refuses to present eradication as a credible promise. The main revision opportunity is precision: define the terms, source the empirical claims, and make authorization, detection, privacy, and publication choices auditable. Keep the provisional solution material, but ensure it remains visibly contestable rather than silently becoming the standard.

**Final stance: refine; proceed to full drafting with the evidence and scope boundaries above made explicit.**
